# Software systems

EAR members rely on software systems to share information and collaborate online. The software we choose to use day-to-day can impact our ability to collaborate effectively, who is enabled to participate, our operating budget, and switching costs.

Members can request software systems support in [Zulip]() or via [email]().

## Selecting software systems

Factors to consider when evaluating software that may be maintained by members of the EAR collective include:

Qualities we look for

* **Data sovereignty** — who owns and controls access to the data?  
* **Ease of migration** — can we *realistically* move our data to a new provider?  
* **Usability** — how flexible is the system in making the information legible to me?  
* **Digital accessibility** — how inclusive is the tool to people with various levels of ability or disability?  
* **Attention** — does the system respect my attention as a volunteer, or does it demand more of me than I have to give in order to get value from it?  
* **Justice** — How does the company treat individuals and organizations with less resources? Do they care? Do they have programs to lift folks up? How does the organization treat its people?  
* **Ease of onboarding** — Can someone with limited bandwidth start working in a reasonable amount of time?  
* **Pricing model** — is the pricing model practical for the number of members who ought to have access?  
* Labor practices  
* **Maintenance cost** — Will the cost of this tool have a downstream influence on deciding who can contribute?  
* **Admin labor** —  
* **Software maturity** — (stable \+ supported?)
