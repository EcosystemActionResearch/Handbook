# Glossary

## General terms
* **Ecosystem:**  There are many overlapping ecosystems within the context of the public interest internet technology ecosystem. When we say "ecosystem", we generally use it in a broad sense. An ecosystem could be understood as, "something (such as a network of businesses) considered to resemble an ecological ecosystem especially because of its complex interdependent parts." — [Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/ecosystem). With this in mind, try to be specific with your use of "ecosystem" and contextualize it. Are you talking about a microblogging ecosystem, a technology ecosystem, a business ecosystem, a network ecosystem, or something else?

* **Action research:** Action research can be understood as, "a process that combines action and reflection to address real-world problems, aiming to create practical, actionable knowledge through identifying gaps and seeking solutions. It is commonly utilized in fields such as education, social work, health, and organizational change to foster improvement over time." — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/social-sciences/action-research). We use action research methods to encourage critical reflection and mutual learning, to meet complex problems and develop a habit of systemic co-inquiry and long-term thinking used to inform practice.

* **Public interest technology:** “Public Interest Technology refers to the study and application of technology expertise to advance the public interest in a way that generates public benefits and promotes the public good, particularly for those members of our society least well served historically and today by existing systems and policies.” — [PIT Lab, Stanford](https://pitlab.stanford.edu/pit)

## Organizational structure

* **Operating model:** defines the shape of the organizational unit known as EAR
* **Steering:** defines and protects EAR’s commitments  
* **Services:** the resource generation and distribution layer to enable autonomous work.  
* **Programs:** EAR-supported initiatives that engage in action research and to foster cooperative [research and imagination](agreements/research-imagination/index.md) in service of innovation.

## Artifacts

* **Resource:** Resources provide important or load bearing context about EAR standard operating procedures. Resources make processes, structures, or rituals legible and accessible to EAR members.  
* **Agreement:** Agreements are policy documents that shape how we work together at EAR. The process for how we ratify and evolve agreements will be defined in a decisions agreement.  
* **Guide:** Guides explain how EAR members get stuff done. Guides are independent from agreements, in that they provide examples and standard operating procedure examples that make it easy to pick up and apply policy defined in agreements to everyday scenarios.  
* **Proposal:** Proposals are conversation starters that diagnose a problem, describe an opportunity area, and propose both guiding policy and an action plan to make it happen. The purpose of a proposal is to solicit feedback and collaboration to introduce a new policy, spark a change, or evolve how we work.
