## Stages

Member projects inquire within overlapping contexts. From exploratory research and discovery to understand the current landscape and challenges, and foundational problem and opportunity framing, to generative, communicative, evaluative, and applicative studies—all lenses help build toward shared understanding and innovation in the ecosystems we work in.

### How to begin

This isn't a comprehensive list of research methods at EAR. Rather it describes ways in to identify and pick a method. It encourages speaking with subject matter experts (e.g. UX researchers, ...) and working together to select appropriate methods. 

It starts with asking... What are you seeing? What is the current landscape? What is important?


See: [How to begin](how-to-begin.md) guide for tips on getting started.

### Exploratory

Identify the current landscape, known unkowns, stakeholder and user environmental factors, and assumptions about needs before deciding what to research or envision next.

Activities: Contextual inquiry, stakeholder and user interviews, discovery mapping workshops.

Potential deliverables: Problem framing, initial empathy mapping, high-level ecosystem mapping, current-state journey mapping.

### Foundational

Understand how users conceptualize a given domain, core architecture, current vision.

Activities: Jobs to be done (JTBD) studies, mental model diagramming, alignment-persona development, card-sorting, competitive usability evaluation.

Potential deliverables: Insights and recommendations report, user experience expert analysis, design principles, opportunity areas.
